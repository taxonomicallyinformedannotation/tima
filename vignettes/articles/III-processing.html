Scoring and network consistency

During weight_annotations(), candidate structures are scored in three steps:

  1. Initial spectral score (weight_bio):
    • SIRIUS CSI:FingerID log-scores (<= 0) are mapped to (0, 1) via a logistic transform (K = 100, scale = 20) and optionally modulated by SIRIUS Zodiac formula confidence.
    • When both library MS2 similarity and SIRIUS scores are present, they are combined via spectral_fusion ("mean", "corroborated", or "noisy_or").
  2. Biological source weighting (weight_bio & clean_bio):
    • Taxonomic matches are scored across all 15 ranks from domain to variety (including infraorder, subfamily, subtribe, subgenus, and subspecies), with universal core metabolites (domain == "Biota") receiving the biota score.
    • Network neighbor consistency (clean_bio) weights each neighbor’s chemical class vote by its edge similarity score rather than treating weak and strong edges equally.
  3. Chemical class weighting (weight_chemo):
    • ClassyFire and NPClassifier agreement with the component’s consensus chemical class is combined with the spectral and biological scores.
    • Each weighted combination tracks per-row evidence coverage (fraction of total weight backed by non-NA evidence in [0, 1]), supports optional Empirical Bayes shrinkage (na_prior = "mean"), and can apply a smooth Cauchy penalty for RT and m/z errors (compute_rt_mz_penalty()).

Avoiding duplicate final candidates across retention times

A single compound (2D InChIKey) cannot elute at two different retention times (> 0.05 min apart) on the same chromatographic gradient. Co-eluting ions of the same peak ([M+H]+, [M+Na]+, or in-source fragments grouped by 1D complete-linkage RT clustering so every cluster has diameter <= 0.05 min) can share the same top candidate, but distinct chromatographic peaks cannot.

When two peaks at different retention times initially rank the same structure 1st—typically isomers sharing an exact mass or a similar MS2 spectrum—TIMA picks the best overall combination of (peak, candidate) pairs across the run:

  • MS2 beats MS1-only: Peak F1 (2.00 min) matches Quercetin by MS2 (0.92), while peak F2 (8.50 min) only matches Quercetin by MS1 (0.78) with Kaempferol ranked 2nd (0.74). F1 keeps Quercetin at rank 1, and F2 promotes Kaempferol to rank 1.
  • Best overall pair, not first-come-first-served: Peak F1 scores 0.90 for IK_A and 0.89 for IK_B, while peak F2 scores 0.88 for IK_A and 0.20 for IK_B. Giving IK_A to F1 would force F2 onto a poor match (0.20, total 1.10). TIMA instead assigns IK_B to F1 (0.89) and IK_A to F2 (0.88), giving a much better combined score (1.77).
  • Ties are never broken at random:
    • If two peaks have identical candidate scores for two isomers, the peak with the smaller RT or m/z error gets the higher-scoring isomer.
    • If the losing peak has two tied runner-up candidates, both are promoted to rank 1 together.
    • If two peaks have 100% identical scores and errors across all metrics, both candidates are kept tied at rank 1 on both peaks.
  • Reported in the output tables: Every promotion is noted in annotation_note (full and filtered tables) and note (mini table), for example: Promoted to rank 1: cross-RT duplicate InChIKey IK_A assigned to feature F2 (or tied with feature F2 on an exact tie).
